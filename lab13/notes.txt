1. Without the lock, why do the letters mix?
Every letter is written with its own write(1, p, 1), so one line costs
31 system calls, 30 letters and 1 newline. Between any two of them the
other process can also call write, and with 3 CPUs (make qemu uses
-smp 3) both processes really run at the same time. So the console gets
the letters of both processes in turn, the "AbAbAb" in my output.

2. Which two lines of mix.c make one line a critical section?
"if (uselock) sem_down(3);" before the inner for loop and
"if (uselock) sem_up(3);" after it. Semaphore 3 starts at 1, so it works
as a lock. The process that passes sem_down(3) writes its whole line,
and the other one sleeps in sem_down(3) until sem_up(3).

3. Why "while (s->value == 0)" and not "if"?
wakeup(s) wakes all processes that sleep on s, but sem_up adds only one
unit. The first one that gets the spinlock takes it, the others find 0.
Also a process that was not sleeping can take the unit first. In my
"mix lock" runs the parent took semaphore 3 again for all its 5 lines
before the child got it once. With "if" the woken process would make the
value negative and enter together with the other one.

4. What would go wrong if sem_down slept holding the spinlock?
sem_up must take the same spinlock before it can add 1 and call wakeup.
If the sleeper kept the spinlock, sem_up would spin in acquire forever
and the sleeper would never be woken, a deadlock. Also xv6 does not
allow giving up the CPU while holding a spinlock (acquire turns off
interrupts), and the scheduler panics in that case.
