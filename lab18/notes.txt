What exactly does thread_switch save and load? Why is ra the register
that makes execution continue in the other thread?
thread_switch saves 14 registers of the current thread into old->ctx,
ra, sp and s0 to s11, 112 bytes in total. Then it loads the same 14
registers from new->ctx. Only callee-saved registers are needed, because
thread_switch is called like a normal function, so the compiler already
keeps the caller-saved ones (a0-a7, t0-t6) on the thread's own stack.
The program counter is not saved. The last instruction ret jumps to the
address in ra, and ra was just loaded from the next thread. For a thread
that already ran, ra points right after its call of thread_switch in
thread_schedule, so it goes on with its loop. For a new thread, ra is the
start of its function, set by thread_create (thread_a is at 0x11c), so
the first ret jumps into thread_a on its own stack.
